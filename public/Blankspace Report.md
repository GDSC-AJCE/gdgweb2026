# Blankspace General Report

## Introduction
[cite_start]Blankspace is a student-led tech community that originated within the 2021-2025 batch of Computer Science and Engineering on September 27, 2022[cite: 10]. [cite_start]Unlike traditional student organizations such as IEEE, ACM, GDSC, or FOSS, Blankspace operates without predefined roles or a formal hierarchy[cite: 10].

[cite_start]Its core philosophy is to foster a collaborative and inclusive environment where students from any department can participate equally, without the oversight of a faculty advisory committee[cite: 11]. [cite_start]Run entirely by students, Blankspace's mission is to promote technological innovation, teamwork, and personal growth through hands-on project development, skill-building sessions, and community-driven events[cite: 12].

[cite_start]The community focuses on connecting like-minded individuals to build, learn, and share knowledge while maintaining a relaxed and informal culture within the core team and membership[cite: 13]. [cite_start]In 2025, Blankspace thrived as an independent community dedicated to nurturing talent and promoting technological collaboration across disciplines with more than 900 community members and 16 core members[cite: 14].

---

## History and Formation

### The Origin of Blankspace
[cite_start]The concept of Blankspace emerged from a desire to create a student-run platform that could operate independently of faculty oversight, focusing on creativity, collaboration, and peer-driven learning[cite: 17]. [cite_start]The idea was first proposed by Adhil Salim, a student from the Department of Computer Science and Engineering (2021-2025 batch), who envisioned a community where students could explore and share their passion for technology without the constraints of traditional academic structures[cite: 18].

[cite_start]Blankspace began as a small WhatsApp group of fewer than 10 members, created to work on joint projects and exchange ideas[cite: 19]. [cite_start]The name "Blankspace" was originally suggested by Alan Thomas Shaji, a fellow student from the same batch[cite: 20]. [cite_start]Over time, the name evolved into "Blankspace," with the lowercase 's' symbolizing the community's focus on filling the blank spaces of students[cite: 21].

### Expansion and Growth
[cite_start]What began as a modest group of friends working on shared projects quickly expanded into a multi-departmental community, attracting members from various academic disciplines[cite: 23]. [cite_start]As the community grew, it became evident that a core team was needed to oversee operations, coordinate events, and manage the increasing membership[cite: 24]. [cite_start]This core team was formed, with former members taking on leadership roles to ensure the smooth running of Blankspace[cite: 25].

### The Merger with Conelce
[cite_start]In the same year, another tech community known as Conelce was formed by students with similar goals and objectives[cite: 27]. [cite_start]Recognizing the synergy between the two groups, Conelce merged with Blankspace under the same name, expanding the core team to include members from both communities[cite: 28]. [cite_start]This collaboration strengthened Blankspace's presence and allowed for greater diversity in the projects and initiatives undertaken[cite: 29].

## Faculty Involvement
[cite_start]On November 29, 2023, Prof. Jayakrishna V from the Department of Computer Science and Engineering joined Blankspace as the faculty advisor, providing support and guidance while preserving the community's student-led nature[cite: 31]. [cite_start]Prof. Jayakrishna's involvement has added valuable insight to Blankspace without compromising the independent spirit of the community[cite: 32].

[cite_start]In 2025, Prof. Ashwin Nandakumar from the Department of Computer Science and Engineering joined Blankspace as the new faculty advisor[cite: 33]. [cite_start]His involvement brings fresh perspectives and guidance, continuing the tradition of faculty support while preserving the community's independent, student-led nature[cite: 34].

## Organization Structure
[cite_start]Blankspace's organizational model stands out due to its lack of predefined roles and traditional hierarchies[cite: 36]. [cite_start]The community operates as a flat, student-driven collective with a focus on collaboration and participation[cite: 37]. [cite_start]To maintain structure and coordination, a core team was established to manage operations, plan events, and ensure smooth communication among members[cite: 38].

### Core Team Composition
[cite_start]The core team consists of students from various departments, originally formed by members who contributed to the community's early development[cite: 40]. [cite_start]After the merger with Conelce, the core team expanded to include members from both communities[cite: 41]. [cite_start]The team plays a pivotal role in organizing workshops, facilitating project development, and guiding new members[cite: 42].

[cite_start]Core team members are not assigned rigid roles; they collaborate dynamically based on the community's needs and ongoing projects[cite: 43]. [cite_start]This flexible system encourages everyone to contribute based on their skills and interests, making Blankspace a unique model of student-led governance[cite: 44].

### Roles and Responsibilities
[cite_start]Although there is no formal hierarchy, core members take on various responsibilities based on their strengths and experiences[cite: 46]. Key tasks include:
* [cite_start]**Event Organization:** Planning and executing workshops, project meetups, and tech talks[cite: 48].
* [cite_start]**Project Facilitation:** Helping members collaborate on coding, design, and development projects[cite: 49, 50].
* [cite_start]**Community Engagement:** Promoting a collaborative environment where members can freely share ideas and knowledge[cite: 51].

### Faculty Involvement Role
[cite_start]Despite being a fully student-driven community, Blankspace is supported by faculty advisors[cite: 53]. [cite_start]On November 29, 2023, Prof. Jayakrishna V (JK) joined as the first faculty advisor[cite: 54]. [cite_start]In 2025, Prof. Ashwin Nandakumar from the Department of Computer Science and Engineering joined as the new faculty advisor[cite: 55]. [cite_start]Their involvement is as core members of the community rather than in a traditional oversight role, ensuring that students retain control over decision-making processes[cite: 56]. [cite_start]Their guidance helps Blankspace align with academic and institutional values while preserving the community's independence[cite: 57].

## Challenges and Solutions
[cite_start]As with any growing organization, Blankspace has faced its share of challenges[cite: 101]. [cite_start]However, the community's flexible and adaptive nature has allowed it to overcome these hurdles[cite: 102].

### Challenges Faced
1.  [cite_start]**Scaling and Coordination:** As the community expanded, managing larger and more diverse groups from various departments became increasingly complex, especially in coordinating events, projects, and member contributions[cite: 104].
2.  [cite_start]**Maintaining Engagement:** With rapid growth, keeping members consistently engaged proved challenging[cite: 105]. [cite_start]Initial interest often waned, making it difficult to sustain active participation[cite: 106].
3.  [cite_start]**Resource Constraints:** As a student-led organization, Blankspace faced limitations in funding and technical support, hindering the ability to host large-scale events or enhance its technical infrastructure[cite: 107].

### Solutions Implemented
1.  [cite_start]**Delegated Responsibilities:** To address coordination challenges, tasks within the core team were distributed more efficiently, with specific members responsible for areas like event management, technical oversight, and communication, improving operational efficiency[cite: 109].
2.  [cite_start]**Incentivizing Participation:** To boost engagement, Blankspace introduced interactive events such as coding competitions, project showcases, and hands-on workshops[cite: 110]. [cite_start]Regular updates and community announcements via social media kept members informed and engaged[cite: 111].
3.  [cite_start]**External Support:** Blankspace collaborated with other student organizations and communities, securing shared resources, conducting various events together[cite: 112]. [cite_start]Faculty advisor Prof. Jayakrishna V (JK) also provided additional logistical support when needed[cite: 113].
